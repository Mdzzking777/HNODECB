AFM04 state-space coupling / compensation notes
================================================

Purpose
-------

This note records two structural compensation risks in the AFM state-space
formulation:

1. ks and cs can be coupled / compensated.
2. x3 and the NN force model can be coupled / compensated.

These two points are not merely numerical optimizer issues. They come from the
state-space formulation and the fact that some variables are not directly
measured in the experimental setting.

This document is also written as a background note for external discussion
with ChatGPT / literature search. Therefore it explicitly states the model
setting, what is measured, what is unknown, and what current engineering
methods are being used.


0. AFM state-space setting
--------------------------

The AFM system is modeled with state variables

    x1: tip displacement / measured trajectory-related state
    x2: velocity-related state
    x3: internal sample deformation / latent internal state

A simplified state-space form is

    x1dot = x2

    x2dot = (Fd * cos(omega * t) - c * x2 - k * x1 + Fts) / m

    x3dot = (-Fts - ks * x3) / cs

where Fts is the tip-sample interaction force. In AFM04, Fts is represented by
a neural model, usually a KAN:

    Fts_raw = KAN(x1, x2, x3)

and the effective force used in the ODE RHS may include additional force-chain
modifiers, such as soft mask:

    Fts_eff = soft_mask * g_nn * Fts_raw

Measured / known information:

    x1 and x2 are treated as experimentally available trajectory information.
    x2dot can be obtained or approximated from the measured trajectory.
    drive force, cantilever parameters, and time are known.

Unknown or optimized information:

    x3 is not directly measured.
    ks and cs are unknown mechanistic parameters.
    KAN parameters are unknown.
    Fts is not directly measured, but can be indirectly constrained through
    the state-space equation for x2dot.

Important distinction:

    Fts is not directly observed, but it is indirectly anchored by measured
    x1/x2/x2dot dynamics.

    x3 is an internal state and is not directly observed in the experimental
    setting.

    Therefore, a low trajectory loss does not automatically imply that x3,
    ks, cs, or KAN_raw have been uniquely identified.


1. ks / cs compensation
-----------------------

In the AFM model, the internal sample state satisfies a relation of the form

    x3dot = (-Fts - ks * x3) / cs

or equivalently

    cs * x3dot + ks * x3 + Fts = 0

Here ks and cs do not appear as two isolated quantities. They jointly control
the internal relaxation dynamics of x3:

    ks controls the elastic/restoring contribution through ks * x3.
    cs controls the time-scale/damping contribution through cs * x3dot.

Because x3 and x3dot are not directly measured, different combinations of
ks, cs, x3 trajectory, and Fts can sometimes produce similar observed
x1 / x2 / x2dot behavior. Therefore a low rollout loss does not automatically
prove that ks and cs have been uniquely or physically identified.

This is the ks / cs compensation risk:

    A wrong ks can be partly compensated by cs.
    A wrong cs can be partly compensated by the learned x3 trajectory.
    The observed trajectory may still look acceptable even when the internal
    mechanical parameters are not close to ground truth.

This is especially important when using prestage2 / stage2light to rank
mechanistic grid candidates. A candidate with lower train loss may still be a
compensated solution rather than the physically closest mechanistic solution.


2. x3 / NN compensation, not simply Fts compensation
---------------------------------------------------

The second compensation risk is between the latent internal state x3 and the
neural force model.

In the current KAN/AFM force chain, the effective force used by the RHS can be
viewed schematically as

    Fts_eff = soft_mask * g_nn * NN_raw(x1, x2, x3)

The important point is that the NN is not learning in an abstract, fixed input
space. It is evaluated along the rollout trajectory. If the rollout x3 drifts,
then the NN is being queried at a different set of x3 values. The NN can then
learn a force map that works on this wrong x3 trajectory while still producing
acceptable x1 / x2 / x2dot losses inside the training window.

Therefore the compensation is not simply:

    Fts_pred vs Fts_true

The deeper compensation is:

    x3 trajectory + NN_raw force map

against the observed dynamics.

This distinction matters. Even if Fts_eff is reasonably anchored in part of the
training window, the learned NN_raw may still not represent the true force law
over the physical state space. It may instead represent a force law adapted to
the model's own compensated x3 trajectory.

In short:

    Fts_eff can look locally reasonable.
    x3 can still be wrong.
    NN_raw can still be learning a compensated force family.


3. Why this matters for W0
-------------------------

W0 is the first-contact window. Our post-processing analyses suggest that W0 is
more fragile for extrapolation than later windows: information related to x3dot
is harder to infer from only (x1, x2, x3), and similar observed states can
correspond to more variable internal dynamics.

This means W0 is more vulnerable to both compensation mechanisms:

    ks / cs compensation can hide in the internal x3 dynamics.
    x3 / NN compensation can produce a locally useful force map that is not
    robust outside the training window.

Soft mask helps by suppressing unphysical far-field force responses, and the
x2dot / force-anchoring style losses help constrain Fts_eff. However, these do
not fully remove the identifiability problem. They reduce one compensation path;
they do not prove uniqueness of ks, cs, x3, or NN_raw.


4. Practical implications
-------------------------

The following points should be treated as working principles for AFM04, KFT,
prest2, and stage2light analysis:

1. Low train loss is not sufficient evidence of physical correctness.

2. ks/cs closeness, x3 behavior, Fts_eff behavior, and NN_raw behavior should
   be interpreted separately.

3. A candidate can win by train loss while still being a compensated
   mechanistic solution.

4. Fts anchoring helps, but it does not by itself guarantee that x3 and NN_raw
   are physically meaningful.

5. Future designs that pass effective x3dot information into the force model
   may reduce the x3 / NN compensation risk, but they must be handled carefully
   because any x3dot-like input generated by rollout is itself predicted and can
   introduce new compensation paths.


5. Terminology
--------------

ks / cs compensation:

    Compensation between the elastic and damping parameters in the internal
    x3 dynamics.

x3 / NN structural compensation:

    Compensation between the unobserved internal state trajectory x3 and the
    learned NN_raw force map.

Fts_eff:

    The effective force actually used by the RHS after all force-chain
    modifiers, such as soft mask and scaling.

NN_raw:

    The raw neural force output before force-chain modifiers. This object can
    be coupled to the learned x3 trajectory even when Fts_eff appears locally
    plausible.


6. Current strategies and remaining uncertainty
-----------------------------------------------

For ks / cs compensation, the current AFM04 strategy is an empirical screening
strategy rather than a rigorously proven identifiability solution.

The intended logic is:

1. stage1pluslight uses a mechanistic grid over ks / cs together with NN seeds.

   The original expectation is that a mechanistic grid closer to the true ks/cs
   should have better overall robustness across different NN seeds. In other
   words, even if individual NN seeds fluctuate, the near-truth mech grid should
   show better average behavior.

2. This expectation is limited in practice.

   The NN seeds are finite and pseudo-random. If we rely on a very large number
   of seeds to estimate average behavior reliably, the computational cost
   becomes very high.

3. Therefore prestage2 / prest2 is introduced.

   prest2 runs a small number of gradient-based optimization epochs for selected
   mech grid + NN seed candidates. The working belief is that a truly good mech
   grid and its associated strong NN seed should begin to reveal itself during
   these short GBO runs.

Therefore, for ks / cs compensation, the current solution is:

    st1pl coarse mech-grid + seed screening
    followed by prest2 short-GBO exposure.

The principle is that even though compensation may occur frequently, the best
mechanistic grid should still emerge under these screening pressures.


For x3 / KAN-parameter compensation, the situation is weaker.

Soft mask is not a complete solution to this coupling. It constrains the
effective force entering the RHS, especially in far-field noncontact regions.
However, even if the final Fts_eff is correct or locally reasonable, x3 and the
KAN raw parameters may still compensate each other:

    wrong x3 trajectory + compensated KAN_raw parameters
    -> acceptable Fts_eff in the training window

Therefore soft mask can reduce certain unphysical force channels, but it does
not prove that x3 and KAN parameters have been separated or physically
identified.

At the current stage, there is no effective dedicated solution for the coupling
between x3 and KAN parameters.


7. Engineering methods that may mitigate x3 / NN compensation
-------------------------------------------------------------

Although there is no complete solution for x3 / KAN-parameter compensation,
several current engineering methods may partially mitigate or expose the
problem.

1. Soft mask

   Soft mask constrains the effective force used by the ODE RHS:

       Fts_eff = soft_mask * g_nn * KAN_raw(x1, x2, x3)

   Its purpose is to discourage unphysical far-field / noncontact force
   responses. In practice, it reduces the ability of the NN to use restless
   noncontact force as a hidden compensation channel.

   However, soft mask does not prove that x3 and KAN_raw are physically
   separated. Even if the final effective force Fts_eff is correct, x3 and KAN
   parameters may still be coupled.

   Core unresolved concern:

       Even if the final output Fts is correct, x3 and KAN parameters may still
       be compensating each other.

2. Force anchoring through the x2dot equation

   Since

       m * x2dot + c * x2 + k * x1 - Fd * cos(omega * t) - Fts = 0

   the measured x1/x2/x2dot information can indirectly anchor Fts_eff. This
   makes it harder for arbitrary NN force outputs to survive if they violate
   the observed cantilever dynamics.

   However, this still anchors the effective force, not necessarily the
   decomposition into:

       x3 trajectory + KAN_raw force map

   Therefore, force anchoring helps constrain Fts_eff but does not fully solve
   x3 / KAN identifiability.

3. Full rollout training

   The model is evaluated through rollout rather than pure one-step supervised
   fitting. This means a compensated x3 / KAN solution must remain dynamically
   self-consistent over a time window.

   This is useful because unstable or unphysical compensation may reveal itself
   through rollout error.

   However, a self-consistent compensated solution can still exist.

4. Optimizer improvements: Adam + backtracking, LBFGS + strong Wolfe

   KFT and AFM04 now use more controlled gradient-based optimization,
   including Adam/AMSGrad with backtracking and a custom LBFGS path with strong
   Wolfe line search.

   These methods improve numerical stability and reduce the chance that the
   optimizer itself creates misleading behavior.

   However, optimizer improvements do not remove structural non-identifiability.
   They can help us find a better local solution, but if the model admits a
   compensated basin, the optimizer may still converge there.

5. Diagnostic post-processing

   Full-horizon rollout plots, Fts reconstruction plots, x3-related diagnostics,
   and window extrapolation tests help reveal when a solution is only locally
   valid inside the training window.

   These diagnostics do not solve the coupling, but they make it visible.


Summary of current status
-------------------------

For ks / cs coupling:

    There is an empirical strategy:
    st1pl mech-grid + NN-seed screening, followed by prest2 short-GBO exposure.

    But this lacks strict academic proof that the true mech grid must emerge
    under finite pseudo-random NN seeds and short GBO screening.

For x3 / KAN-parameter coupling:

    There are mitigation tools:
    soft mask, force anchoring, full rollout training, optimizer stabilization,
    and post-processing diagnostics.

    But these tools do not fully prove that x3 and KAN parameters have been
    decoupled or physically identified.

    The main unresolved issue remains:

        correct or plausible Fts_eff does not guarantee correct x3 or physically
        meaningful KAN_raw parameters.
