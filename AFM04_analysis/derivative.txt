AFM04/AFM05 derivative-loss state choice and gradient implications
=================================================================

Purpose
-------
This note records the distinction between the current rollout-state x2dot loss
and a possible partially observed-state alternative. It also explains why
substituting observed x1 and x2 into the known stiffness and damping terms can
change not only the magnitude of the x2dot loss, but also the sign and direction
of its parameter gradient.

This is a conceptual and diagnostic note only. No AFM04, AFM05, or AFM06
training implementation is changed by this document.


1. State dependence of the tip-side RHS
---------------------------------------
Suppressing normalization and range-penalty details, the AFM04/AFM05 tip-side
equation has the form

    f2(t, x; theta)
      = a_act(t)
        - (k/m) x1
        - (c/m) x2
        + (1/m) Fts_hat(x1, x2, x3; theta),

where

    Fts_hat
      = g_NN M(x1, x3; theta_mask)
        r_KAN(x1_norm, x2_norm, x3_norm; theta_KAN).

Consequently:

    * x1 enters f2 directly through the stiffness term;
    * x2 enters f2 directly through the viscous/damping term;
    * x1 and x2 also enter indirectly through the learned force model;
    * x3 has no separate linear term in f2, but affects f2 through Fts_hat
      (including the KAN and, where applicable, the soft mask).

The unavailable x3 trajectory is therefore the reason that AFM04/AFM05 cannot
construct a fully observed-state evaluation of the complete learned RHS without
using unavailable or oracle information.


2. Current rollout-state x2dot loss
-----------------------------------
The current derivative prediction is evaluated along the model rollout:

    x2dot_hat_roll(t_i)
      = f2(t_i, x1_hat(t_i), x2_hat(t_i), x3_hat(t_i); theta).

Its normalized residual and loss can be written as

    r_roll,i
      = [x2dot_bar(t_i) - x2dot_hat_roll(t_i)] / s_x2dot,

    L_x2dot_roll
      = (1/N) sum_i r_roll,i^2.

This definition is dynamically self-consistent: every RHS term is evaluated at
the same model-predicted state. However, the residual simultaneously contains:

    * error caused directly by x1_hat - x1_bar;
    * error caused directly by x2_hat - x2_bar;
    * error in the learned Fts_hat;
    * error transmitted through the latent x3_hat trajectory.

The x1 and x2 state losses already penalize the first two errors. The rollout
x2dot loss therefore applies an additional, dynamically coupled penalty to them.


3. Possible partially observed-state alternative
-------------------------------------------------
AFM04 has observed/simulated x1_bar and x2_bar. These values could be inserted
only into the known stiffness and damping terms while retaining the predicted
state in the learned force branch:

    x2dot_hat_mixed(t_i)
      = a_act(t_i)
        - (k/m) x1_bar(t_i)
        - (c/m) x2_bar(t_i)
        + (1/m) Fts_hat(x_hat(t_i); theta).

The corresponding residual is

    r_mixed,i
      = [x2dot_bar(t_i) - x2dot_hat_mixed(t_i)] / s_x2dot.

Let

    e1_i = x1_hat(t_i) - x1_bar(t_i),
    e2_i = x2_hat(t_i) - x2_bar(t_i).

Then

    r_mixed,i
      = r_roll,i
        - [(k/m)e1_i + (c/m)e2_i] / s_x2dot.

This operation will often reduce L_x2dot because it removes the direct
stiffness- and damping-induced contributions of the rollout state errors. A
smaller loss under this definition does not, by itself, demonstrate that
Fts_hat or the complete rollout dynamics are more accurate.

This is a mixed-state RHS rather than a fully observed-state collocation
constraint: the known mechanical terms use observed x1 and x2, whereas the
learned force still depends on the predicted state, including x3_hat.


4. Why the gradient can change sign and direction
-------------------------------------------------
For an arbitrary trainable parameter theta_j,

    dL_x2dot/dtheta_j
      = (2/N) sum_i r_i (dr_i/dtheta_j).

Changing from r_roll to r_mixed changes both factors in this expression.

First, the residual changes by

    -[(k/m)e1_i + (c/m)e2_i] / s_x2dot.

Even if the residual Jacobian were unchanged, this change can alter the
weighted inner product, make a gradient component cross zero, and therefore
reverse its sign.

Second, the gradient-propagation paths change. In the rollout-state definition,
the derivative contains the direct paths

    (k/m) d(x1_hat)/dtheta_j
      + (c/m) d(x2_hat)/dtheta_j,

in addition to the total derivative through Fts_hat and x3_hat. In the mixed
definition, the observed x1_bar and x2_bar are constants, so these two direct
paths disappear.

Therefore, replacing the rollout states in only the known mechanical terms can:

    * increase or decrease individual gradient components;
    * reverse the sign of individual gradient components;
    * rotate the full gradient vector in parameter space;
    * change its cosine similarity with the state-loss gradients;
    * change which parameter groups receive the strongest optimization signal.

It is incorrect to regard the alternative merely as a numerically smaller
version of the same x2dot loss. It defines a different optimization objective.


5. Potential benefit and limitation in AFM04/AFM05
--------------------------------------------------
Potential benefit:

    The derivative term would no longer repeat the direct stiffness and damping
    consequences of x1 and x2 rollout errors. Its gradient could consequently
    focus more strongly on the learned-force and latent-state branches, and the
    conflict with the state-loss gradients might be reduced.

Limitations:

    * x3 remains unobserved, so the force branch is not evaluated at a fully
      observed state;
    * the mixed RHS is not the same vector field used by the rollout;
    * a low mixed-state x2dot loss can coexist with a poor rollout trajectory;
    * state-error propagation can be hidden rather than corrected;
    * a lower numerical value does not establish better force recovery or
      mechanistic identifiability.

For these reasons, the change should not be justified solely by observing a
smaller x2dot loss.


6. Difference from the clean AFM06 observed-state construction
--------------------------------------------------------------
AFM06 has no latent x3 and its KAN inputs are only x1 and x2. Hence the complete
RHS can be evaluated pointwise at the observed state:

    x2dot_hat_obs(t_i)
      = f2(t_i, x1_bar(t_i), x2_bar(t_i); theta).

Because all KAN inputs are observed, this is a coherent observed-state
vector-field/collocation constraint. In AFM06, the resulting residual is also
algebraically equivalent to the learned effective-force error evaluated along
the observed trajectory.

AFM04/AFM05 cannot obtain the same clean construction merely by substituting
x1_bar and x2_bar into the stiffness and damping terms, because x3_bar is not an
available training input.


7. Current decision and possible future diagnostic
---------------------------------------------------
No implementation change is made at present. The partially observed-state
definition remains a hypothesis for later testing.

If it is evaluated in the future, the decisive comparison should use the same
saved model and trajectory and report, for both definitions:

    * L_x2dot;
    * the full x2dot-loss gradient norm;
    * parameter-group gradient norms;
    * cosine similarity between x2dot-loss and x1/x2 state-loss gradients;
    * changes in gradient-component signs;
    * rollout loss and trajectory quality after controlled optimization.

Only this comparison can establish whether the alternative removes harmful
gradient conflict or merely makes the auxiliary derivative objective easier.
